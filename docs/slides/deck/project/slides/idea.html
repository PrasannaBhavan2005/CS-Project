3 · Our idea

Treat each video frame as one unit and place it where it finishes first

1

Predict

For each new frame, estimate when it would finish on each link.

2

Place or split

Send the whole frame on the earlier link; split a large frame across both only if that finishes sooner.

3

Rescue

If a link stalls (no progress for one timeout), resend its unfinished part on the other link.

Built inside the sender, on top of multipath QUIC: each frame part is its own QUIC stream.

The scheduler we propose, F2, works at the level of frames. It predicts each link's finish time for the frame, sends the frame whole on the link that finishes first, splits only when splitting is faster, and rescues a part stuck on a link that has gone quiet. The pieces are known ideas (ECF's completion estimate, DEMS's balanced split, stream-aware multipath QUIC); what is new is putting them together at the teacher's side and measuring them with deadlines and video decoding rules. We also test the same logic over two separate ordinary QUIC connections (A1) to see whether multipath QUIC is needed at all. F1 (place only, no split or rescue) is the ablation.

5 · How we test it

Same video, same links, every scheduler: measured, not assumed

Testbed

Two emulated links on one laptop, with delay, speed and outages set exactly; later replaying real two-operator recordings.

Video

A real 60-s NPTEL blackboard lecture, encoded like a live stream at 2.6 Mbit/s, 25 frames per second.

Score

Share of frames shown on time (within 250 ms) and freeze time (gaps ≥ 190 ms).

Five predictions, written down before any run

One link is often not enough (H1) · today’s schedulers waste capacity when delays differ (H2) · our scheduler freezes less (H3) · it needs no special protocol (H4) · it helps most with big keyframes (H5)

Everything runs in emulation so every scheduler sees exactly the same conditions: two network namespaces joined by two links, each with its own delay, speed and a 200 ms buffer, and scripted outages. Teammates will record real two-operator traces at three sites and we replay them. A frame counts as shown only if it and every frame it depends on arrived within 250 ms of capture; a freeze is a gap of at least 190 ms (the WebRTC rule at 25 fps). Hypotheses with thresholds are fixed in the proposal: H2, for example, says MinRTT and ECF reach under 80% of an ideal link's usable capacity when the delays differ 3 times or more. Each comparison uses 8 paired runs: with 6 main comparisons, Holm's first threshold is 0.05/6 = 0.0083, and 8 runs is the fewest whose best Wilcoxon p-value (2/2^8 = 0.0078) can pass it.

2 · What exists today

Multipath protocols split the video packet by packet

Multipath QUIC can already use two links in one connection. A scheduler picks a link for every packet: the faster one (MinRTT), turn by turn (round-robin), both (redundant), or by expected finish (ECF).

Commercial bonding boxes (LiveU) are closed; research that sends whole frames (MoMQ, 2026) works at a relay, not at the teacher’s phone.

The weak spot

Packets of one video frame land on both links. The frame can only be shown when its last packet arrives, so the slow link decides when every frame appears.

Existing schedulers were designed for bulk data, where only total throughput matters. For live video a frame is useful only when complete and on time. If one frame's packets are spread over a 30 ms link and a 150 ms link, the frame waits for the 150 ms link. ECF, the best transport scheduler (Lim et al., CoNEXT 2017), estimates completion time but still per packet, and does not know where frames begin and end. We implemented ECF ourselves as the strongest baseline.
